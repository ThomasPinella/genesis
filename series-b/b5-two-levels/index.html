Two levels

As in B4, most balls have four spots and some, the caps, have one. Now a cap's spot is bigger: it holds its partner twice as hard as two four-spot balls hold each other. Every cap is +, so caps cover − spots, and a ball wearing its caps is a molecule that still shows its leftover spots. Those can only make weak bonds, joining molecules into looser clumps. Do molecules last while clumps form and break, a second level built from what the first leaves over?
